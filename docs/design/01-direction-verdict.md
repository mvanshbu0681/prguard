# Design Direction — Verdict

> Produced 2026-09-22 by a 33-agent research workflow (12 research + 12 fact-check + 3 directions + judge + 4 blueprints + critic).


## Scores

| Direction | First impression | Differentiation | Product truth | Feasibility | Dashboard fit | Longevity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **COLD LIGHT** | 7 | 9 | 10 | 8 | 10 | 9 | **53** |
| **Exhibit** | 7 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 8 | **51** |
| **BLAST DOOR** | 9 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 6 | **41** |

## Verdicts

### COLD LIGHT

WINNER. The only direction where the visual system and the product thesis are the same object. A spectrometer/lab-bench register is genuinely unoccupied in a category drowning in indigo gradients, mint accents and mascots, and — decisively — it is uncopyable by anyone whose architecture cannot execute, because the entire language is built to display captured artifacts rather than illustrate claims. Severity rendered as a calibrated reading with the .15/.35/.55/.75 thresholds printed on the axis is the single most on-message visual in the whole set: M2's claim is 'this is arithmetic, not an adjective', and a ticked instrument scale says that without copy. Refusing green anywhere is a real point of view that also solves a real problem (distinguishing PRGuard's gate from GitHub's check three pixels away). Dashboard fit is best-in-class because the marketing texture becomes a functional graticule and three components ship twice. Its weakness is honest and named by its own author: it has no floor. With no imagery, no 3D and one accent hue, quality lives entirely in the grid, the 1px details and a 72px monospace headline that is unforgiving if set badly. Scores 7 on first impression rather than 9 because a judge scanning at three seconds gets typography and terminal text, not a gasp — which is why it needs the grafts below.

### Exhibit

CLOSE SECOND, AND THE SOURCE OF THE BEST SINGLE IDEA IN THE ENTIRE SET. Exhibit and COLD LIGHT are siblings — same Google Sans Code mono display face, same cold near-black, same registration marks, same two-pane base/head terminal hero, same instrument cyan, same Web Crypto tamper, same stacked contribution bar, same refusal of WebGL. Convergence from two independent proposals on the same corpus is itself strong evidence the direction is right. Exhibit loses on two counts. First, it lacks a SUBSTRATE: COLD LIGHT's graph paper is what stops a large dark field from reading as an empty div and what makes the dashboard's charts sit on something rather than float, and Exhibit has no equivalent. Second, its severity treatment is the same bar without the printed threshold ticks carrying the argument. Where it beats COLD LIGHT outright: the PROVENANCE FOOTER (`GET /v1/reviews/{id}/result → findings[0].proof`) under every visual, which is the cheapest and most differentiating device anyone proposed; the contenteditable canonical_json (tampering by typing beats tampering by button); the 4-second HOLD in the hero replay where nothing moves and the judge actually reads; Wilson intervals on the accuracy metrics; and the best build sequencing of the three — the hero works at the end of day one and every subsequent day is additive, so any day can be the last day.

### BLAST DOOR

HIGHEST CEILING, WRONG BET. It is the only direction that delivers the owner's stated cinematic preference and the only one that produces a genuine three-second gasp — a monumental backlit shut steel door is a different object in a different room from every competitor. Its signature beat, THE SLAM, is also the best-directed 2.9 seconds anyone wrote. But four things sink it. (1) Differentiation is weaker than it looks: vault/gate/blast-door is the most worn metaphor in security branding, and condensed industrial uppercase is a known register — it is distinctive versus CodeRabbit, not distinctive in absolute terms. (2) Product truth is the real problem. The gate is M4 literalised, but a gate says 'we block things', and PRGuard's actual thesis is the opposite emotional claim: fewer, truer, proven findings, and a system honest enough to refute itself. Proof-by-execution, computed severity and semantic fingerprint all end up as ordinary sections beneath a metaphor that does not express them. (3) Feasibility is the highest-variance of the three — the author's own risk list opens with 'if the CSS leaf face reads as two grey rectangles there is no fallback position', plus a pinned 140vh slam, a pinned 170vh pipeline, a WebGL god-ray canvas at 0.0.81, and a Blender escape hatch. (4) Dashboard fit is 6 by its own admission: the conic rim light survives on exactly one element and everything else is a generic dark admin. Its ideas are worth stealing; its spine is not worth building.


## Winner: COLD LIGHT

BUILD COLD LIGHT'S SPINE. Graft Exhibit's provenance device and build sequencing onto it, and graft exactly three things from BLAST DOOR: the keyboard-triggered replay, the DISPROVEN counter-demo, and the weight curve on the one heavy element. Nothing else from BLAST DOOR ships.

WHY THIS IS THE CALL. All three tie at 16/20 on the two highest-weighted axes, so the decision falls to product truth, where COLD LIGHT wins outright (10 vs 9 vs 7). COLD LIGHT and Exhibit independently converged on the same answer from the same corpus — cold near-black, Google Sans Code as a MONOSPACE DISPLAY FACE, registration marks, a two-pane base/head terminal hero, instrument cyan, a weighted contribution bar, Web Crypto tamper, zero WebGL. That convergence is the strongest signal in the whole exercise. COLD LIGHT wins the tiebreak because of two things Exhibit does not have: a substrate (graph paper) and printed threshold ticks on the severity scale.

=== THE SPINE (COLD LIGHT, unchanged) ===
Tokens: ink-0 #080A0D page through ink-12; alpha hairlines for all decorative borders; ink-7 #595E64 (3.03:1) as the floor for any MEANING-BEARING border — input outlines, selected rows, focus rings. Instrument cyan --signal #2ECEDD for execution results, --alarm #F64F47 as the only chromatic gate state, --marker #E7B551 for caution. Severity as a strictly monotone CHROMA ramp (0.008 → 0.045 → 0.105 → 0.160 → 0.205) so it survives greyscale and CVD as an intensity ramp, not five hues. NO GREEN ANYWHERE — gate clear is achromatic with a cyan underline.
Substrate: graph paper (two repeating linear-gradients, 8px minor / 64px major at 0.028 alpha) + one bounded inline feTurbulence grain at 0.035. This is non-negotiable and is what stops a large dark gradient from banding and from reading as an unstyled div.
Depth: one rung on the ink ladder + 1px hairline + `inset 0 1px 0 oklch(1 0 0 / 0.06)`. Zero drop shadows. Backdrop-filter in exactly two places (sticky header, ⌘K palette).
Ornament: four 8px L-shaped corner registration marks per panel, 0.10 → 0.22 alpha on hover. HARD RULE (taken from Exhibit): registration marks appear ONLY on evidence-bearing frames — never on buttons, chips or dashboard panels, or the one ownable detail becomes a template tic.
Type: Google Sans Code (OFL, v7.001, latest tag — not 7.000) as display AND mono. Inter Variable for prose only. Zero code ligatures by construction, slashed zero by default, tracking exactly 0 on all mono data. Headline hard-capped at 8 words, hand-checked at 1280/1440/1920 against the literal string.
Motion: two easings (--ease-instrument, --ease-snap), five quantised durations, linear for every scrub, nothing opacity:0 in the stylesheet, and the deliberate 0ms colour flip on verdicts paired with a 180ms scale(1.045)→1. That instantaneous flip is the most important motion decision in the project and must not be softened.

=== GRAFT 1, FROM EXHIBIT: THE PROVENANCE LINE (highest-value graft) ===
Every evidence frame on the marketing site AND every panel in the dashboard carries a footer strip in 11px mono --text-dim naming the endpoint the data came from: `GET /v1/reviews/4e8b1d0/result → findings[0].proof`. It costs one span. It is the single most differentiating device anyone proposed, because it tells a judge that what they are looking at HAS A SOURCE, not a designer — which is the positioning sentence rendered as a UI element. In the dashboard it doubles as a genuinely useful debugging affordance. Pair it with COLD LIGHT's section index (`/ 05 — COMPUTED SEVERITY`) as the panel HEADER strip, so every frame is bracketed: index above, provenance below. Do NOT import Exhibit's full `EXHIBIT 01 · PROOF · finding a91f3c2e · captured 2026-09-19T14:22:07Z` header — it is one field too many and fights COLD LIGHT's restraint. Keep index + category + identifier; drop the captured-at timestamp to the footer beside the provenance path.

=== GRAFT 2, FROM EXHIBIT: THE 4-SECOND HOLD ===
COLD LIGHT's hero sequence ends at t=4200ms and goes idle. Add Exhibit's discipline: after the PROVEN chip stamps at t=2200ms and `→ blocks merge` lands at t=2400ms, HOLD for 4.0 seconds with absolutely nothing moving except the heartbeat dot. This is where a judge reads. Without it the sequence is a demo of animation; with it, it is a machine returning a result and then sitting still, which is the whole brand.

=== GRAFT 3, FROM EXHIBIT: TAMPER BY TYPING ===
Replace COLD LIGHT's "tamper with event 1428" BUTTON with Exhibit's contenteditable `canonical_json` field, debounced 120ms, recomputing the whole chain on every keystroke via `crypto.subtle.digest`. A judge changing `"severity": 0.81` to `0.80` with their own keyboard and watching the rail physically break with a 6px gap converts an abstract cryptographic claim into something they personally verified. Keep COLD LIGHT's break design: visible gap, everything below desaturated, header flips to `chain.verified = false · broken_at_seq = 1428` with a 0ms colour snap. Add Exhibit's expected/computed hash diff with the first differing nibble highlighted, and a `↻ restore` button.

=== GRAFT 4, FROM EXHIBIT: WILSON INTERVALS + ALWAYS-VISIBLE DEFINITIONS ===
On section 03 and on /accuracy, every metric renders its value, its DEFINITION as an always-visible 13px subtitle (never a tooltip), its n, and a Wilson confidence interval as a hairline error bar. The numbers do NOT count up. Half-disclosing is worse than not disclosing for a product whose thesis is proof, and an error bar next to `execution_verified_precision 0.91 (n=312)` is a differentiator no competitor offers.

=== GRAFT 5, FROM BLAST DOOR: KEYBOARD-TRIGGERED, DETERMINISTIC REPLAY ===
Bind the hero sequence to the `P` key and the pipeline sequence to a visible ▶ control, both paused-timeline-first. Build them keyboard-driven BEFORE wiring scroll. Two payoffs: the money shot is frame-identical on demand when a judge says "show that again", and if the scroll scrub slips on the cut list you still have a complete demoable moment. This is the cheapest insurance in the plan.

=== GRAFT 6, FROM BLAST DOOR: THE `D` KEY DISPROVEN COUNTER-DEMO ===
The strongest idea in BLAST DOOR and it transplants perfectly. Press `D` and the identical bench sequence runs on a different finding whose head-SHA run PASSES. Terminal pane B shows `1 passed in 0.61s` in cyan, the claim strikes through, the chip stamps `DISPROVEN` in cyan OUTLINE with a `refuted by execution` caption, and the verdict rail reads `gate stays clear · 0 proven · 1 self-refuted`. Ten seconds of the system publicly losing an argument with itself, on stage, on demand. No competitor can perform this. It also pays off COLD LIGHT's best palette decision — proven and disproven sharing the cyan hue because both are execution results — by making the reason legible in motion rather than needing a legend to explain it. (Still ship the legend.)

=== GRAFT 7, FROM BLAST DOOR: TWO SMALL THINGS ===
(a) Name the dashboard's second sidebar group "Governance" (Suppressions / Audit / Accuracy). One string, sells M4 every time anyone glances at the rail. (b) Add a third easing token `--ease-mass: cubic-bezier(0.33, 0, 0.12, 1)` — slow start, long accelerate, hard arrest — used for exactly ONE thing: the scan line's travel down the proof bench. Everything else stays on the two published curves. A curve with real weight on the one element that traverses the whole panel is what separates "animated" from "actuated".

=== WHAT THE SYNTHESIS DELIBERATELY DOES NOT TAKE ===
No blast door, no god rays, no @paper-design shader, no preserve-3d scene, no Blender AVIF, no second pinned section, no condensed uppercase display face, no conic rim light. COLD LIGHT ships ONE pinned section (the 9-stage pipeline) and nothing else scroll-jacks.

=== BUILD ORDER (Exhibit's sequencing, COLD LIGHT's cut list) ===
Day 0 (0.5d): tokens in OKLCH, gamut-clamped and APCA-verified with the official `apca-w3` package. NOTE: the real APCA body-text floor is Lc 75, not Lc 60 — COLD LIGHT's --text-body at Lc -78.2 clears it, Exhibit's ink-11 at Lc 75.3 barely does, so use COLD LIGHT's values. Graph paper + grain + the Surface primitive with hairlines and registration marks. ~60 lines of CSS carrying most of the expensive read.
Day 1 (1d): THE PROOF BENCH, static, at frame 0 only — both terminals rendered, verdict stamped, provenance line present, no animation. At the end of day 1 the hero already works. That property is what makes this plan safe.
Day 2 (1d): the hero replay timeline (paused/keyboard first, scroll last), the P and D bindings, the fallback handshake (CSS-armed 1600ms force-reveal, disarmed only after JS confirms the animation runtime loaded).
Day 3 (1d): the severity instrument face — contribution bar, threshold ticks, input table, list-row sparkline. Then the chain rail + contenteditable tamper.
Day 4–5 (2d): the 9-stage SVG pipeline, sequenced as static geometry (3 breakpoint sets from one TS constants file with an `elbow(from,to)` helper) → live-mode driver → scroll scrub LAST. The live driver is needed for the dashboard regardless, so the scrub is the only part that can slip.
Day 5–6 (2d): dashboard shell, findings table, run view, finding detail, suppression flow, audit, accuracy — reusing ProofBench, StageRail and ContributionBar.
Day 6 (0.5d, NON-NEGOTIABLE): reduced-motion second state, `[data-contrast="high"]` projector variant, a11y pass (live regions, focus ring, scroll-margin-top for WCAG 2.4.11 which is Level AA not AAA), offline fixture mode, and a 90-second MP4 of the proven-bug moment on the desktop.
CUT ORDER UNDER PRESSURE: (1) pipeline scroll scrub, (2) the agent-caught section, (3) bespoke icon marks, (4) light-theme audit export. NEVER cut the Proof Bench, the severity instrument, or the tamper control — those three ARE PRGuard.

=== STACK DECISIONS THE PLAN SHOULD LOCK ===
One Next.js 16.3.6 app, two route groups, no monorepo, no Multi-Zones (cross-zone links are hard navigations and the hero CTA → dashboard click is the whole narrative). Node 22 LTS. React pinned exactly (19.2.0 is safe; 19.3.0 is also fine since no R3F is in the tree). Tailwind 4.3.3 CSS-first, `@theme inline` for next/font vars, `@theme static` for any token the pipeline SVG reads via getComputedStyle. shadcn `init -b radix` — and note the Base UI package was RENAMED to `@base-ui/react`, so the widely-cited `@base-ui-components/react` is npm-deprecated. GSAP 3.15.0 + ScrollTrigger on the marketing route only (~46 KB gz, not 70); its npm licence field is Webflow's non-SPDX free-text string so add one CI allowlist entry. Motion on the dashboard only via LazyMotion. Polling at 1.2s with backoff, never SSE. anser for ANSI, never xterm.js. @tanstack/react-table pinned ^8, NOT v9 (ESM-only, no migration guide). Wrap @git-diff-view behind one `<PRGuardDiff>` component. Shiki with 6–10 languages imported individually, never the barrel. Demo from a production domain, never a preview URL (bytecode caching and pre-warming are production-only).


## Explicitly rejected

- THE BLAST DOOR ITSELF — the door leaf, the corridor, the seam, the lintel, the two-leaf slam. Its own author calls it 'the whole direction' with no fallback position, it is the most worn metaphor in security branding (vault/gate/airlock), and it says 'we block things' when PRGuard's actual thesis is 'we make fewer, truer claims and refute our own'. Kill the object; keep the slam's timing discipline and the D-key counter-demo.
- ALL WEBGL AND ALL SHADERS — @paper-design/shaders-react GodRays (pinned 0.0.81, a pre-1.0 package that ships breaking changes under 0.0.x), Three.js, R3F, any canvas. Of the elite dark dev-tool set surveyed, essentially none ship a WebGL hero; CodeRabbit's 'cinematic 3D' is a conic-gradient rim light over flat panels at 2–12% white. A scene that stutters on the venue projector actively costs the judging moment while communicating nothing about proof.
- THE OWNER'S 3D/REALISTIC PREFERENCE, AS LITERALLY STATED. Delivered instead as depth stacking, the graph-paper substrate, the 1400ms scan, the 0ms verdict flip and one pinned pipeline. This must be SOLD in one prepared sentence, not quietly ignored (see open decisions).
- IBM PLEX SANS CONDENSED UPPERCASE AS THE DISPLAY FACE — condensed at 6.5rem with −0.028em collides the moment a headline or label runs long, and the industrial-signage register belongs to the blast-door concept that is being dropped. Google Sans Code at 520 is the call.
- BERKELEY MONO — disqualified on terms before price. The commercial licence states 'Commercial licenses are not compatible with open-source apps' and restricts commercial use to UI elements with terminal apps explicitly called out as generally disallowed. PRGuard's hero IS a terminal view. Real cost for a small team is $790+ (Startup $495 + Web Fonts module $295), web fonts are never bundled, and at the 8–50 tier the web module becomes a $45/month subscription.
- GEIST (SANS OR MONO) — CodeRabbit's only typeface, plus Vercel, cubic, Snyk, basement.studio, Dimension, trigger.dev. Arriving in Geist makes you the cheaper version of the incumbent. Geist Mono additionally has an l/1 collision (open issue #102) needing ss03.
- INTER AS A DISPLAY FACE — Linear, Raycast, Neon, Graphite, Resend, Supabase, Modal and Warp all ship it. Prose only, and note that the commonly-copied cv01/ss03 settings only affect the digit 1 and quotes/commas; cv11 (single-storey a) is the setting that actually changes texture in English.
- RADAR / SPIDER CHART FOR THE SIX SEVERITY INPUTS — radar encodes by area, so it systematically misrepresents the one number the product's credibility rests on, and it is the single most over-used 'AI scoring' visual. The weighted contribution bar with printed threshold ticks is both more honest and more legible at 320px.
- GREEN PASS STATES AND A GREEN CHECKMARK — the most commoditised signal in CI, and it sits three pixels from GitHub's own check in a PR tab. Gate clear is achromatic on a cyan tint. Refusing green is a differentiator and a legibility win.
- MAKING DISPROVEN RED — encodes intellectual honesty as failure and inverts the product's proudest behaviour. Proven and disproven share the cyan hue because both are execution results; differentiate by fill vs outline plus a mandatory glyph.
- AMBER FOR DISPROVEN — a verifier caught that FAR 25.1322 defines amber/caution as 'requires immediate flightcrew awareness and subsequent response'. A self-refuted finding requires no response at all. Amber belongs on INCONCLUSIVE, which genuinely does need a human to decide.
- A SECOND PINNED SECTION — BLAST DOOR's 140vh slam plus 170vh pipeline is ~310vh of scroll before section 03. One pinned section on the whole site, and it is unpinned below 1024px because iOS Safari's collapsing toolbar produces the jump-and-stick artefact that instantly reads as broken.
- CSS SCROLL-DRIVEN ANIMATION AS A MECHANISM — `animation-timeline: view()` is Baseline 'limited' with Firefox absent from stable entirely. A Firefox judge sees elements parked at their 0% keyframe, i.e. a blank page. Use it only inside `@supports` for a decorative progress rail with a ScrollTrigger fallback beneath.
- RIVE, LOTTIE, dotLottie, SPLINE, VANTA — Rive's real payload is ~896 KB gzipped measured (100.8 KB JS + 795.3 KB WASM), not the ~200 KB commonly quoted, and its free tier cannot export a .riv at all. dotLottie is ~485 KB gz of WASM plus an After Effects dependency. Spline has no LICENSE file and no license field (proprietary by default) at ~479 KB gz. Vanta's last publish was 2022.
- A LOGO WALL, TESTIMONIAL CARDS, ARR COUNTERS OR INVESTOR LOGOS — there are no customers, and a thin or fabricated social-proof row is read as a student project instantly. The 'WE WERE WRONG' disproven section and the tamper-able hash chain occupy that slot and are stronger proof than logos would be.
- GLOBAL ASCII / DITHER POST-PROCESSING — reported ~900% YoY search growth means it will read as 2026-vintage by mid-2027, and it annihilates headline legibility. Exhibit's masked x-ray lens variant is the only defensible form and it is not worth the schedule.
- A PRELOADER OF ANY KIND, INCLUDING THE 'HASH CHAIN VERIFYING ITSELF' ONE — it costs LCP, risks an impatient judge, and the page is already interactive underneath. The chain verification belongs in section 07 where it can be interacted with.
- CUSTOM CURSORS, MAGNETIC BUTTONS, CURSOR TRAILS AND ANY SITE AUDIO — 2 of 21 current Awwwards SOTD winners ship a custom cursor and 0 of 21 ship audio. On a governance tool a hijacked pointer directly costs Usability, worth 30% of the Awwwards rubric, and it fights the text selection people need when reading terminal output.
- MOTION+ (£299) — GSAP 3.13+ ships SplitText and ScrambleText free, and AnimateView has since graduated out of Motion+ early access. There is nothing to buy.
- REACT FLOW / @xyflow ON THE MARKETING PAGE — pan, zoom, drag and a required stylesheet you then spend an afternoon disabling, plus an attribution badge or $169/mo. Cloudflare, with unlimited budget, hand-writes its lines. The 9-stage diagram is hand-authored inline SVG with pathLength="1" on every edge.
- MAGIC UI AnimatedBeam — its gradient pins y1/y2 to "0%" under userSpaceOnUse, so the light wipes horizontally across the bounding box rather than travelling the path. Structurally wrong for a vertical spine, and it emits one full-container SVG per beam. Its #ffaa40→#9c40ff default is also the loudest 'assembled from a registry' tell there is.
- shadcn `init --base base-ui` — Base UI's stable package is `@base-ui/react`; the widely-cited `@base-ui-components/react@1.0.0-rc.0` is npm-deprecated. Radix is the boring correct choice for a five-day build and every third-party registry still assumes it.
- @tanstack/react-table v9 — ESM-only, weeks old, no published migration guide, and every recipe and model prior is v8. Pin ^8.21.3.
- A PRICING PAGE — the category is uniformly per-seat with a 'Custom' column, and the claim that usage pricing is unoccupied is false (Qodo bills base-plus-credits, Greptile gates on credits, CodeRabbit bills per agent-minute). Omit pricing entirely, as GitHub Copilot's code review page does.

## Open decisions

- THE OWNER ASKED FOR DARK/GRADIENT/3D/CINEMATIC AND THIS DELIVERS INSTRUMENT-GRADE FLAT. Do we sell the refusal, or concede one shader? RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: sell it, with this sentence — 'Of 22 elite dark dev-tool sites surveyed, essentially none ship a WebGL hero; CodeRabbit's cinematic 3D is a conic gradient over flat panels at 2–12% white; and for a product whose claim is that it executes code, a floating abstract object is the visual form of an unfalsifiable assertion.' If the owner will not budge, the ONE concession that does not break the direction is a single static Dithering surface at ≤5% opacity behind section 02, pinned to an exact version, behind a WebGL2 feature check with a static fallback — never in front of text, never load-bearing. Decide this in the first hour; it changes nothing downstream if settled early and costs two days if reopened on day four.
- NINE SECTIONS OR SEVEN? Estimated ~6,500px plus ~2,700px of pinned scroll is longer than the category-confident ~5,000–7,000px. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: build nine, cut to seven only if it tests long, in this order — drop section 08 (the agent-caught story) first, then fold section 04's proof-state legend into section 03. Never cut 03 (we were wrong), 05 (the instrument face) or 07 (identity + flight recorder).
- DOES THE DISPROVEN COUNTER-DEMO SHIP, GIVEN IT NEEDS A REAL DISPROVEN FINDING IN THE FIXTURE SET? RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: yes, and make it a backend requirement on day zero — seed the demo corpus with at least one genuinely disproven finding and one delivery-ladder fallback (an inline_nearest and a commit_status), or the S8 ladder shows four lit rungs and no story. A demo showing 100% proven is worse than not shipping the section.
- WHO OWNS THE CANONICAL-JSON SPEC? The tamper control shows a FALSE break the moment the browser's serialisation diverges from the backend's by one byte of key order, whitespace or unicode escaping — the most self-refuting failure available to this product. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: write the canonicalisation rules into a shared spec file on day zero, test both sides against identical fixtures in CI, and treat a failing fixture as a build-breaking error. Backend owns the spec; frontend consumes it.
- MONOSPACE HEADLINE AT 72px — commit or hedge? Mono is wide and unforgiving. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: commit, but set the literal string at 1280/1440/1920 on the actual demo machine on day one, hard-cap the headline at eight words, and confirm Google Sans Code's variable wght axis renders (if it falls back to a static 400 cut the entire hierarchy flattens). If it looks like a terminal banner rather than a headline after that test, drop to 56–62px rather than changing typeface.
- WHEN DOES THE PROJECTOR VARIANT GET BUILT? The ink-0/ink-1/ink-2/ink-3 steps collapse into one flat black on a washed-out venue display and the 0.028-alpha graph paper disappears entirely. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: build `[data-contrast="high"]` (every hairline promoted to solid ink-7 #595E64) on day one alongside the token file, not in the day-six polish block — it is four lines of CSS if the tokens are semantic and a rewrite if they are not. Switch to it before walking on stage.
- ONE ANIMATION LIBRARY OR TWO? GSAP on marketing (~46 KB gz) plus Motion on the dashboard (~20 KB gz) means two mental models and a real chance someone imports GSAP into a dashboard component. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: ship both, split by route, and enforce the boundary with an ESLint `no-restricted-imports` rule rather than discipline. GSAP's pin + snap + invalidateOnRefresh genuinely have no Motion equivalent; Motion's layoutId genuinely has no GSAP equivalent without hand-writing a Flip choreography per case.
- DO WE PUBLISH self_caught_false_positive_rate ON THE MARKETING SITE? It invites 'so you're wrong 14% of the time?' on stage. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: yes, prominently, with the answer rehearsed in one sentence — 'that is the rate at which the system caught itself BEFORE the claim reached your PR, which is the inverse of a defect' — and with every caveat rendered as always-visible body text, never a tooltip. Half-disclosing is worse than not disclosing for a product whose thesis is proof.
- EXACT REACT PIN. RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: 19.2.0 with a package.json `overrides` block. The usual reason to pin (R3F 9.7.0 peers `>=19 <19.3`) does not apply since no R3F is in the tree, so 19.3.0 is also defensible — but Motion's AnimateView requires 19.3+, so if that component is wanted, pin 19.3.0 instead. Decide once, pin exactly, commit the lockfile; do not use a caret on any core dependency for a five-day build.
- LIGHT THEME AND PRINT STYLESHEET FOR THE AUDIT CHAIN AND ACCURACY REPORT, PLUS AN 'EXPORT EVIDENCE' ACTION? RECOMMENDED DEFAULT: yes if the schedule holds, and it is the FIRST thing cut if it does not. 'Here is the PDF you hand your auditor' is a beat nobody else in this category can show, and a dark-only audit trail is a tell that the audit trail is a prop — but it doubles the theming work on two surfaces and print CSS is easy to leave half-done.
